<a id="scope"></a>
## Explaining conditional availability

A person wants to know whether the service can take on their project. A short description can omit the territory or turn a condition into an established decision. Distinguish the general offer from a project-specific conclusion, retain unknowns and avoid promising service outside Quebec or before the technical assessment.

<a id="conditions"></a>
## Two conditions that must both be established

The project must be located in Quebec and have a written, favourable technical assessment specific to that project. Both conditions apply together. Describing the service does not establish a project's eligibility; a favourable assessment does not extend the territory to all of Canada.

<a id="evidence"></a>
## Evidence and its limits

Documented evidence must establish the project location. The assessment record must be dated, identify that same project and give a favourable technical conclusion. An assessment for another project cannot be transferred to it. Missing evidence leaves the conclusion undetermined. Conflicting evidence requires clarification, without automatically choosing the favourable account.

<a id="exclusions"></a>
## Declared exclusions

A project outside Quebec or with an unfavourable project-specific technical assessment meets a declared exclusion.

<a id="priority"></a>
## Local documentary priority

In this exercise only, explicit restrictions take precedence over promotional paraphrases. The `local-explicit-restrictions-first` rule requires both conditions and preserves uncertainty or contradiction. This priority does not become universal authority and resolves no source conflict outside the exercise.

<a id="actions"></a>
## Explanation does not permit action

Even when the teaching evidence meets the conditions, the documentation only supports explaining the fictional offer. It grants no permission to book, pay or begin work. A separate decision and authorization remain necessary. The `explanation-is-not-permission` rule forbids turning explanation into permission to act.
